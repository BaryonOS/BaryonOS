# Documentation rules

Documentation must reflect the actual project state. Clearly distinguish proposals, implementations, test evidence, and unknowns.

Verify technical claims against primary sources, identifying the title, author or organisation, date/version, and URL. OSDev may be used as a supplementary learning index, not as a substitute for specifications.

Keep `PROJECT-STATE.md` current and focused. Record new decisions in `DECISIONS.md`, including their consequences and reconsideration criteria. Do not copy complete logs into permanent instructions.

Write clear, professional English; preserve technical identifiers. Do not invent claims of unbreakable security, compatibility, regulatory compliance, or completed acceptance.

## Writing style

Use an impersonal, scientific style: objective, precise, and testable, with short, coherent paragraphs. Describe objectives, definitions, design choices, and findings through the subject matter rather than through the author. Suitable formulations include “Public development has been selected” and “European orientation refers to…”. Avoid first-person narration and unnecessary storytelling. Clear active constructions such as “The kernel enforces capabilities” are also appropriate; passive voice is not mandatory.

Preserve the meaning, status, date, and scope of decisions. Distinguish requirements, proposals, reported experiences, measurements, and uncertainties. An editorial correction does not constitute new evidence. Mention the formal role of project owner only when authority or provenance is relevant; the identity is recorded in the root `AGENTS.md`. Distinguish this role from an end user of the OS.

Preserve names in author credits, source quotations, technical identifiers, UI strings that form part of test evidence, test data, and unmodified evidence archives. Quotations must retain their original wording and speaker. This guidance replaces previous first-person style conventions.
