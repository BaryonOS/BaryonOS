The question that sharpened the project

European preference for greater digital autonomy does not automatically produce operating-system migration. In the 2026 Digital Decade Eurobarometer, 58% of respondents agreed that they would be willing to switch to an EU-based digital service provider, even at slightly higher cost. The survey did not ask whether they would install a European operating system.

The resulting research question: if European control, privacy and security are valued, why does that preference not translate into mass operating-system switching—and what would a European OS have to offer before people could change without giving up speed, reliability, familiar work or a polished future-facing experience?

The reviewed evidence points to more than origin or privacy: necessary applications, hardware, data migration, learned habits, collaboration, support and long-term maintenance all affect switching. Baryon OS therefore treats a privacy claim or independent kernel as a foundation, not as a complete reason to adopt the product.

Mission

Develop and test an independent European-oriented operating system in which people can work normally, understand where their data goes and control what apps may do—without accepting an outdated, disconnected or technically diminished experience.

Vision

A person should be able to move to Baryon OS through a safe, understandable route and find a fast, refined and familiar desktop. Everyday apps, hardware, internet access, recovery and maintenance should work as one coherent product. Material should keep work findable and portable between replaceable tools. Personal storage should remain local or explicitly encrypted before it leaves a device. Optional AI should assist on-device within selected context.

What makes this a research programme?

Each part of the vision is treated as a question that needs evidence. Market and scientific studies inform hypotheses about switching, information organisation, privacy decisions, visual first impressions, notification load, accessibility and human–AI interaction. Technical contracts translate those hypotheses into testable boundaries. Native QEMU, physical hardware and user studies remain distinct evidence levels.

What the mission does not claim

The 58% result is not an OS adoption forecast. European origin does not prove privacy, security or quality. The project has not demonstrated mass demand, parity with established platforms or a supported daily-use release. Those outcomes require functional compatibility, sustained voluntary use and completed product acceptance.
