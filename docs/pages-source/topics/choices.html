Why keep the desktop familiar?

Changing operating systems already asks people to move files, learn differences and check whether their tools still work. Familiar setup, windows, taskbar, search and shortcuts reduce avoidable learning. Baryon OS adds its own visual identity and new capabilities where they provide value instead of renaming every ordinary action.

Why build an independent kernel?

The kernel is where memory, processes and access to shared resources are controlled. Developing that foundation inside the project makes the authority model part of Baryon OS rather than a theme placed on another desktop. This is technically important, but it is not enough on its own: users still need apps, drivers, recovery and support.

Why organize work around Material?

People often care about the document, photograph or project—not which app currently owns it. Material keeps content, revisions and permissions conceptually separate from tools. Familiar folders, names and search are intended to remain available because research shows that personal organization differs and learned methods still matter.

Why are app permissions narrow?

Opening one document should not expose every file. Installing an app should not silently grant network, device or administrator access. Separate, understandable permissions make it possible to replace a tool, revoke one task or choose a different storage location without surrendering unrelated data.

Why is local-first still connected?

Privacy should not mean an isolated or inconvenient computer. Local work remains possible, while normal internet access and explicitly paired storage are product requirements. Remote providers may store client-encrypted objects only after a deliberate choice; they do not become silent fallbacks or content-key holders.

Why is accessibility part of the identity?

People differ in vision, motion sensitivity, reading, attention, hearing and input. One rigid “accessible mode” cannot represent all of those needs. The design uses a usable baseline plus combinable preferences for contrast, motion, typography, stimulus and input, while essential information and safety remain present in every theme.

Why is AI optional and on-device?

An assistant can help with selected material, but it should not require uploading personal context or make ordinary work depend on a model. Inference is therefore required to stay on the device, with separate model downloads, limited context and revocable actions. Suggestions must remain reviewable and correctable.

Why show evidence and unfinished work so explicitly?

A polished prototype can create the impression that a whole OS works. The project therefore labels design, host prototype, native QEMU, physical hardware and human evaluation separately. This lets visitors see both the ambition and the actual boundary without turning research into a product claim.

No single study proves that these choices will make people switch. Together they form research-backed hypotheses and binding design requirements that still need technical acceptance and trials with real users.
