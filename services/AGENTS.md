# System service rules
Read the root instructions and security model. Give each service explicit responsibilities, inputs, permissions, quotas and recovery behaviour.
IPC schemas must support versioning; always validate bounds and provenance. No implicit global access. Check confused-deputy risks and delegation.
Apply docs/LOCAL-PRIVACY-CONTRACT.md **revision 4** and docs/implementation/ENCRYPTED-OBJECT-STORAGE.md. Personal NAS synchronisation uses an explicitly paired NAS owned by the user or by friends/family; LAN is the preferred route, and internet access requires explicit peer/destination rights and actual transport authentication. A user may also optionally connect an **explicitly selected external storage provider** as an untrusted backend for opaque ciphertext objects encrypted exclusively on the client. The provider receives no plaintext, sensitive manifest contents or Nieuw OS content keys, and is never a silent fallback or mandatory OS relay. User keys remain within the defined client/Key Service boundary; NAS administration may delete its own space and ciphertext without guest consent but has no right to read content. Revocation also blocks old sessions, upload commits and resuming synchronisation. The **Security Floor** remains in force when privacy choices are broadened: internet or document access does not grant additional system, key or other-app permissions. The AI inference service has no network permission. The model/update downloader receives no personal data or general NAS credentials. Networking is restricted per service and destination, rather than blocked globally across the OS; only previously granted permissions allow network access. Logs are local and exclude secrets/personal content by default.
Use maintained cryptographic libraries; do not invent primitives/protocols. Entropy, key lifecycle, rollback protection and failure modes are part of the integration.
Test that crashes remain contained and recovery does not silently broaden permissions.
